# Code of Conduct

`md2nativedocx` is small, early-stage, and largely maintained by one person. That makes the tone
this project sets now — while it's still forming — matter more, not less: it's easier to keep a
good norm than to fix a bad one once the project has grown. This document exists so that anyone
opening an issue, filing a PR, or reviewing someone else's code here knows what's expected, and
what happens if it isn't met.

It borrows its structure and enforcement ladder from the [Contributor Covenant][homepage] (v2.1),
adapted to this project's actual size and spaces rather than left as generic boilerplate.

## The short version

Disagree rigorously, not personally. This project has already changed its own scope and name
several times during planning (see `cahier_des_charges.md` §14) and has a written norm of
escalating uncertain calls to a human rather than guessing (see `AGENTS.md`) — arguing hard about
an architecture decision, a rejected PR, or a security trade-off is exactly the kind of disagreement
this project runs on. Arguing at the person who made the call is not, and won't be tolerated
regardless of who's technically right.

## Our pledge

We as members, contributors, and maintainers pledge to make participation in this project a
harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible
disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience,
education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual
identity and orientation.

We pledge to act and interact in ways that contribute to an open, welcoming, and healthy community
— including toward first-time contributors still finding their footing with OOXML, Dagre, or
Pandoc's Lua filters, who should be able to ask a basic question without it being held against
them.

## Expected behavior

- Demonstrating empathy and kindness toward other people.
- Being respectful of differing opinions, viewpoints, and experiences — including disagreeing
  with a maintainer's technical decision, in the open, with reasons.
- Giving and gracefully accepting constructive feedback, on code and on ideas.
- Accepting responsibility and apologizing for mistakes, and learning from them — this project's
  own `TODO.md` documents its bugs and wrong turns in public rather than hiding them; the same
  candor is expected of people, not just of code.
- Focusing on what's best for the project and the people using it, not for any one contributor's
  preference or ego.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Sexualized language or imagery, and sexual attention or advances of any kind.
- Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks — including ones
  disguised as code review ("this is obviously wrong" about a person rather than a diff).
- Public or private harassment.
- Publishing others' private information — a physical or email address, or anything else — without
  their explicit permission.
- Any other conduct that would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting.

## Scope

This applies everywhere this project actually happens: GitHub issues, pull requests and their
review comments, commit messages, GitHub Discussions if enabled, and any other space explicitly
tied to this repository. It also applies when someone is representing the project elsewhere —
speaking about it publicly, or under its name.

Security vulnerabilities are reported separately, through the private channel in `SECURITY.md` —
this document covers conduct, not disclosure.

## Enforcement

This is currently a single-maintainer project — reports get a best-effort response, not a formal
SLA, the same posture `SECURITY.md` takes on vulnerability reports. That will be revisited as the
project grows past one maintainer.

Report unacceptable behavior to **nicolas.bridelance@totetbatet.com**. Every report is reviewed
and investigated, and the reporter's privacy and safety are respected throughout — details are
shared only as far as resolving the report requires.

Project maintainers are responsible for clarifying and enforcing these standards, and have the
right to remove, edit, or reject comments, commits, code, issues, and other contributions not
aligned with this Code of Conduct, explaining why when appropriate.

## Enforcement guidelines

Maintainers will follow these guidelines in determining the consequences for any violation:

### 1. Correction

**Impact**: Use of inappropriate language or other unprofessional or unwelcome behavior.

**Consequence**: A private, written warning explaining the nature of the violation and why the
behavior was a problem. A public apology may be requested.

### 2. Warning

**Impact**: A violation through a single incident or a pattern of smaller ones.

**Consequence**: A warning with consequences for continued behavior — no interaction with the
people involved, including maintainers, for a specified period, across this project's spaces and
adjacent ones (e.g. social media). Violating these terms escalates to a temporary or permanent ban.

### 3. Temporary ban

**Impact**: A serious violation, including a sustained pattern of inappropriate behavior.

**Consequence**: A temporary ban from any interaction or public communication with the project for
a specified period. No public or private interaction with the people involved is allowed during
this time. Violating these terms escalates to a permanent ban.

### 4. Permanent ban

**Impact**: Demonstrating a pattern of violation, including sustained harassment of an individual
or aggression toward a class of people.

**Consequence**: A permanent ban from any public interaction within the project.
